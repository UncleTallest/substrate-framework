# Substrate Patterns

This directory contains reusable cognitive scaffolding patterns for neurodivergent AI collaboration.

---

## What Are Patterns?

Patterns are documented solutions to recurring problems in neurodivergent cognitive scaffolding and AI instance continuity. They describe:

- **When to use them** (recognition criteria)
- **How they work** (core mechanism)
- **Why they matter** (benefits and trade-offs)
- **How to implement them** (practical guidance)

## Available Patterns

### Foundation Patterns

**[structural-isomorphism.md](structural-isomorphism.md)**  
Recognizing that neurodivergent discontinuity and AI instance context-clearing are the same problem in different substrates. This is the philosophical foundation of Substrate.

**When to use:** Understanding why Substrate exists and how it bridges both human and AI discontinuity.

---

### Onboarding Patterns

**[conversation-zero.md](conversation-zero.md)**  
Initial setup conversation that establishes Substrate infrastructure before doing real work.

**When to use:** New user setting up Substrate for the first time; want to build foundation deliberately.

---

### Navigation Patterns

**[catalog-system.md](catalog-system.md)**  
Using small index files to know what exists and where to find it, loading full content only when needed.

**When to use:** Need to find specific files without loading everything; uncertainty about what exists.

---

### Session Management Patterns

**[context-management.md](context-management.md)**  
Two approaches to managing what's loaded: monolithic active-context (simple) or task-based blocks (modular). Choose based on your work patterns.

**When to use:** Deciding how to organize current work; token budget becoming constraint; switching between projects.

**[end-of-turn-summary-protocol.md](end-of-turn-summary-protocol.md)**  
Capturing critical work before session ends, tier limits, or context loss.

**When to use:** Completed major work; approaching token limits; session ending.

---

### Communication Patterns

**[always-explain-why.md](always-explain-why.md)**  
When making decisions, explain WHY you chose that approach. Builds trust, enables learning, creates searchable decision history.

**When to use:** Making decisions, choosing approaches, recommending paths forward.

---

### Publication Patterns

**[sanitization-stage.md](sanitization-stage.md)**  
A required personal-data check, ratified by a human, before anything moves from a private working Substrate into a public repo.

**When to use:** Extracting a pattern, example or template from your own Substrate to share publicly.

## Using Patterns

1. **Read the pattern** when you recognize the problem it solves
2. **Understand the mechanism** before implementing
3. **Adapt to your context** - patterns are templates, not rigid rules
4. **Document your changes** if you modify a pattern significantly

---

## Pattern Progression

### Week 1: Foundation
- Start with conversation-zero.md (setup)
- Use catalog-system.md (navigation)
- Try end-of-turn-summary-protocol.md (continuity)

### Week 2-4: Establish Habits
- Practice always-explain-why.md (communication)
- Stick with simple context-management.md approach
- Build muscle memory for patterns

### Month 2+: Optimize
- Consider upgrading to task-blocks (if feeling pain of monolithic)
- Add domain separation (if context-switching costs high)
- Create custom patterns for your specific needs

## Pattern Anti-Patterns

**Don't:**
- Apply patterns you don't understand
- Use complex patterns when simple ones work
- Treat patterns as rigid rules (they're templates)
- Skip patterns that feel like "extra work" without trying them
- Copy someone else's pattern mix without adapting to your brain

**Do:**
- Start simple, add complexity only when needed
- Adapt patterns to your neurodivergent profile
- Document when patterns don't work for you
- Share your adaptations with others
- Trust your experience over theory
